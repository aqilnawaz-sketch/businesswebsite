## Criminal Law & Bail — /criminal-lawyer-bahawalpur/
**1. What should I do after an FIR is registered against me?**
Get a copy of the FIR and read the sections applied. Do not make statements to the police without a lawyer. If the offence is non-bailable and arrest is
likely, consider pre-arrest bail under section 498 CrPC. Keep documents, messages and witnesses that support your version, and join the investigation
through counsel. → link: guide "What to do after an FIR" (planned).

**2. What is the difference between bailable and non-bailable offences?**
The Second Schedule to the Code of Criminal Procedure classifies each offence. For a bailable offence, bail is a right (section 496 CrPC). For a
non-bailable offence, bail is at the court's discretion under section 497 CrPC. Courts are stricter where the offence is punishable with death,
imprisonment for life or ten years. **[VERIFY wording of the prohibitory-clause sentence]**

**3. How can I get a copy of the FIR?**
The informant is entitled to a free copy under section 154 CrPC. An accused person can usually obtain a copy from the police station or, once the case is
in court, a certified copy from the court. **[VERIFY: mention the Punjab Police online FIR facility only if the owner confirms it is current]**

**4. What are the rights of an arrested person?**
Under Article 10 of the Constitution, an arrested person must be told the grounds of arrest, may consult a lawyer of their choice, and must be produced
before a magistrate within 24 hours. Article 10A guarantees a fair trial. Article 13 protects against being compelled to be a witness against oneself.
A confession made to the police is not admissible under the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984.

**5. When can an accused seek acquittal before the full trial?**
A magistrate may acquit under section 249-A CrPC, and a Court of Session under section 265-K CrPC, at any stage if the charge is groundless or there
is no probability of conviction. During investigation, the police may release an accused for want of evidence under section 169 CrPC.

## Family Law & Khula — /family-lawyer-bahawalpur/
**1. How can a wife claim maintenance?**
By a suit in the Family Court, which has exclusive jurisdiction over maintenance. The court can fix interim maintenance early in the case under section
17-A of the Family Courts Act 1964. **[VERIFY the Punjab consequences of non-payment of interim maintenance before stating them]**

**2. How is a maintenance decree enforced?**
Through execution in the Family Court. Unpaid maintenance under a decree can be recovered by the court's execution process.

**3. How can haq mehr (dower) be recovered?**
A wife can sue for her dower in the Family Court. Prompt dower is payable on demand; deferred dower becomes payable on dissolution of marriage or death,
unless agreed otherwise. **[VERIFY limitation statement before adding one]**

**4. What is the difference between khula and dissolution on statutory grounds?**
Khula is dissolution at the wife's request without proving fault, usually with return of the dower or benefits received, to the extent the court
directs. Dissolution under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939 requires proof of a listed ground, such as cruelty or failure to maintain for
two years. **[VERIFY Punjab 2015 dower-return rule for khula]**

**5. What if the husband lives abroad?**

## Inheritance & Succession — /inheritance-lawyer-bahawalpur/
**1. How is inheritance distributed under Pakistani law?**
For Muslims, shares are fixed by Islamic personal law. For example, a son receives twice the share of a daughter, and a widow receives one-eighth where
the deceased left children and one-quarter where there are none. Shares depend on the full list of surviving heirs and the applicable school of law, so
each case is calculated individually.

**2. What documents are needed for succession proceedings?**
Usually the death certificate, the NADRA family registration certificate (FRC), CNICs of all heirs, and records of the assets: fard or registry for
land, and bank account details for balances.

**3. Can a daughter claim her inheritance if her brothers refuse?**
Yes. Her share is fixed by law. Remedies include correcting the inheritance mutation, a civil suit for declaration, possession or partition, and a
complaint under the Punjab Enforcement of Women's Property Rights Act 2021. Depriving a woman of inheritance by deceitful or illegal means is an
offence under section 498-A PPC.

**4. Can a relinquishment (dastbardari) deed be challenged?**
It can be challenged through a civil suit for declaration and cancellation of the document under the Specific Relief Act 1877. This applies where it was
obtained by fraud, pressure or without proper understanding. The court examines the circumstances in which it was signed.

**5. Who issues a succession certificate or letter of administration?**
In non-contentious cases, NADRA issues them under the Letters of Administration and Succession Certificates Act 2020. Where heirs disagree or the
matter falls outside NADRA's process, the civil court decides under the Succession Act 1925.
